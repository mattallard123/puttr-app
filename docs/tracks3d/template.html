The choice

Four ways Tracks can look in Unity

The game underneath stays the same: one mat putt per shot, the putt's length picks the club, pace sets how far and line sets where. What changes is how much of the course you see and feel.

A · closest to today

Overhead, now with depth

The arcade map you have, drawn as a lit 3D model seen from above. Shadows, dunes and the burn read at a glance; the ball arcs up toward the camera.

  • Same reading of a hole as the RN app
  • Cheap to run on any phone
  • Still feels like a minimap
Effort: low. The Overhead plan camera above.
Recommended

Broadcast golf

Plan from above, then cut to a TV camera behind the ball for the shot: tracer, hang time, a follow camera on the bounce, and a green camera for the putt.

  • Every shot becomes a moment worth watching
  • The booth commentary already exists to sit on it
  • Fits the arcade's stage and lighting work
Effort: medium. This page's Broadcast and Ball follow cameras.
C · most immersive

Player's eye

Stand on the tee looking down the hole, like a simulator. Strong on a TV or iPad on a stand; harder to plan a shot from on a phone.

  • Big wow on the TV app
  • Needs real terrain and trees to convince
  • Line of sight hides hazards
Effort: high. Wants real elevation and art.
D · data-heavy

Real-world terrain

Swap the sketched dunes for survey heights. Scotland and much of the US publish 1 m lidar; satellite imagery could texture the ground.

  • The real Road Hole bunker, the real Valley of Sin
  • Licensing and size per course to work through
  • Pairs with B or C
Effort: per course. A pipeline, then automatic.
The interface

What gets better on screen

The putt is the swing

The mat strip in the shot bar shows exactly what you are about to putt and what it buys you, so the link between the mat and the course is never a mystery.

Read the green in 3D

On the green the camera drops low and slope arrows run across the surface. Pro and Major levels can hide them for a real read.

14 ft, breaking left to right. Your mat putt: 3.5 ft from the right tee.

A scorecard that stays with you

A thin strip under the hole card, filled in hole by hole, with birdies circled and bogeys boxed the way a real card is marked.

The booth, in captions

The two-voice commentary Tracks already plays, shown as a lower third so it lands with the sound off too.

BoothInto the wind on the Road Hole. He'll want to keep that left of the hotel.
BoothLovely. That's found the fairway.
How we would build it

A path in four steps

  1. Course to mesh at loadRaster the same OSM shapes into a ground texture and a height field in Unity, as this page does in the browser. No art per course, so all 67 tracks work on day one.
  2. Broadcast cameras on the arcade stageReuse the arcade's lighting, sky and ball physics. Plan view, tee camera, follow camera, green camera, with the tracer.
  3. Interface passHole card, wind, caddie club with the mat strip, scorecard strip, booth captions. Landscape on tablets puts the shot bar beside the hole.
  4. Real terrain, course by courseStart with St Andrews: lidar heights and the famous holes first (1, 17, 18), then decide if it is worth the pipeline.
