Puttr 2 · first-run tour

Three ways to show a new Player around

Onboarding collects a name, a hand and a goal, then connects the Puttr. After that a new Player is dropped on Home with four tabs and six ways to play, and as of today no sentence explaining any of them. This page is the research on how the best apps handle that moment, and three directions for Puttr, each drawn in the Broadcast system.

Round two, 25 November: all three are redrawn with XP and levels in them, following the XP plan. The changes are summarised under "What XP changes" below.

Research

What the best apps do

The pattern across the strongest examples is the same: nobody tours the interface any more. They get the new user to do the product's one core action, teach the rest at the moment it is needed, and pay the user for getting there.

94% vs 91%Skippers vs readers, task success

Nielsen Norman Group tested upfront tutorial decks with 70 people on four apps. People who skipped the tutorial did as well as people who read it, and rated the apps easier to use.

For Puttr: no swipe-through deck, and no four-stop tab tour before the first putt.

NN/g, mobile tutorials

72% → 16%Tour completion, 3 steps vs 7

Chameleon's benchmark of in-app tours shows completion holding through three or four steps and collapsing by seven. Produktly's 2026 medians agree: 73% for one or two steps, 38% for three to five.

For Puttr: any sequence is three beats at most. Everything else waits.

Userpilot citing Chameleon · Produktly

Do it firstDuolingo, Strava

Duolingo puts a real lesson ahead of sign-up and reported about a 20% lift in daily users from delaying the ask. Strava's first session ignores clubs, feed and challenges and points at one thing: record an activity.

For Puttr: the tour is a putt, then a game. The interface is learned on the way.

Taplytics on Duolingo · UXCam

A real repTonal, Zwift, EA Sports PGA Tour

Hardware and sports titles teach with the equipment in hand. Tonal runs a demo, then a short calibration set. Zwift's first ride earns XP and unlocks gear. EA's Coaching Academy teaches the basics as challenges that pay XP.

For Puttr: the first session is on the mat, and finishing it earns something visible.

Tonal · Zwift · EA

Pull, not pushHelp when the user is there

NN/g separates tips pushed at launch from tips pulled in by what the user is doing, and recommends the second. Game studios say the same: contextual tips, features revealed gradually, not all at once.

For Puttr: a section explains itself the first time it is opened, once, and never again.

NN/g, tutorials in apps · Unity FTUE tips

Show progressChecklists that feel like a game

Getting-started checklists work when they show momentum and fail when they read as chores. Sports games frame the same list as a path with a visible end and a reward.

For Puttr: a checklist has to look like golf, not like setup.

Appcues · GameAnalytics

Source quality: the NN/g study is the only controlled test here. The completion and uplift numbers come from tool vendors and second-hand write-ups, so read them as direction, not as targets. I found nothing usable on Whoop or Oura.

Round two · with XP

What XP changes

The XP plan gives the tour what it lacked: a reward that already has a bar, a place on the final page and a level-up screen. So the three directions now share one system, and the Front Nine no longer needs a prize of its own.

  • Opening Round ends on the XP panel, with the bar 400 short of Level 2.
  • The Front Nine pays once for each hole. Signing the card pays 300 and a card frame. The crest is gone.
  • First Look pays nothing. Its strip on the first game over explains the XP panel.

How fast that is

  • After the Opening Round: 600 XP. The game pays about 100, the round 300, and holes 1 and 2 of the card 200.
  • Level 2 on day two. Two more holes and a game or two cover the last 400.
  • A signed card is about Level 3. The one-time awards add up to 1,500; five or six games in the week add the rest.
  • The flat 1,000 a level is untouched. The early pace comes from one-time awards only.
Approach A

Opening Round

A three-minute guided round straight after onboarding. One putt, one game, then Home. The Coach talks the Player through it like a broadcast rundown.

  • Three beats, shown as a rundown strip under the header: First putt, First game, Your plan.
  • The Coach's voice carries it. The persona voices and lower-third are already in the app's language; the strip shows the same words for anyone with sound off.
  • Nothing is fake. The putt is Free Putt, the game is On Fire, the last stop is the real Home. Scores count.
  • It ends on XP. The first game's final page shows what it paid. The round's own 300 leaves the bar 400 short of Level 2, on purpose.
  • No Puttr connected: the same round plays itself as a demo (the auto-putt the test builds already use), ending on Connect.

Why it could win

It is the Duolingo and Tonal pattern: the Player has made a putt, read it, and finished a game before seeing a menu. It attacks the 31% who never play.

What it costs

It teaches the core loop, not the breadth. Tracks, Quests, Tournaments, League and Online are not covered. It also holds a new Player in a script for three minutes, so Skip has to be honest and always there.

Approach B

The Front Nine

A scorecard of nine firsts, played over the first week. Each hole is one part of Puttr and pays 100 XP once. Sign the card for 300 more and a card frame.

  • One card on Home under today's session, with the nine holes and the next one named. It leaves Home when the card is signed.
  • Self-paced. Holes can be played in any order, on any day. Each one deep-links to the real feature.
  • The hole is a row in the XP panel on game over, and the next hole is one list row under the buttons. No extra strip.
  • Kid profiles get a nine without Online: "Play a four Player game" takes that hole.

Why it could win

It is the only one of the three that covers the whole app and the only one built for day two through day seven, which is where the 45% who play once are lost. A scorecard is native to golf, so the checklist does not read as setup.

What it costs

It explains nothing in the first minute; a Player who never opens the card learns nothing from it. It adds a second progress display to Home for a week, where the XP plan allows only the level chip and the daily putts. The build is smaller than in round one: the award-once logic, the bar and the payoff all come with XP, which leaves the nine triggers and the card.

Approach C

First Look

No tour at all. The first time a Player opens a tab or a way to play, a broadcast lower-third slides up with one sentence about it. Once per place, then gone.

  • Eleven places: the four tabs, the five Play sections besides Local, the first putt read and the first game over, where the strip explains the XP panel.
  • It pays no XP. Level is effort, and reading a caption is not.
  • The app stays live under it. No dimming, no blocked taps. Using the screen dismisses it as well as Got it.
  • One at a time. Never two on a screen, never over a putt in flight, never queued.
  • Settings has "Show first looks again", next to the Puttr Caddie for anything else.

Why it could win

It is the pull pattern NN/g recommends, and it is the direct replacement for the sentences removed today: the same words, shown once and not forever. Smallest build: one component and a seen-list per profile. It works with or without a Puttr.

What it costs

It never gets anyone to the mat. A Player who stays on Home sees one strip and nothing else. It is the quietest of the three, which also makes it the easiest to ignore.

My recommendation

They stack. Start with A and C together, and ship B with XP.

  1. Opening Round is the tour. It is the only one that puts a ball in the hole in the first minute, and that is what every strong example in the research does.
  2. First Look covers what the round skips. It is cheap, and it is where the six sentences removed today belong. With both, the Opening Round's last beat can drop its line about Play and Improve.
  3. The Front Nine is the first week of XP. In round one it was a separate feature for later; with XP it reuses the bar, the panel and the award logic, so it should ship in the same release as levels.
  4. If the Opening Round ships before XP, it ends as round one drew it (straight to Home) and gains the XP page when levels land.
To decide

Questions for the next round

  • Voice. Does the Coach speak in the Opening Round, or is it text only? Voice means new lines recorded for each persona.
  • Order with the Coach first run. The Opening Round uses a Coach before the Player has picked one on Improve. Either the pick moves forward into onboarding, or the round is hosted by a neutral Puttr voice.
  • No Puttr yet. The XP plan says test putts earn nothing, so a demo round cannot move the bar and would end on Connect. Is it still worth building, or does the round wait for the first connect?
  • The nine. The XP numbers are agreed. Are these the right nine holes?
  • The colour of new XP. The XP plan draws added XP in amber. Broadcast keeps amber for time running out and streaks, so it is drawn white here. One of the two needs to change.
  • Home. The XP plan allows the level chip and the daily putts on Home and nothing else. Is the Front Nine card acceptable there for one week?
  • Existing Players. With backfill they start well up the ladder (the plan's example is level 37). My proposal: First Looks only, no round and no card.
  • TV. A TV only has the Play tab and a remote. First Look ports cleanly; the other two need their own design.
