Quests you have to beat

Quests today are a list of ten numbered tiles that all play on the same ramp. Now that the app is in Unity, a quest can be a place: a world with levels, lives, secrets and a boss. Below is what exists, then three ways to rebuild it, each with its level select and gameplay screens.

How it works now
  • Three tiers by difficulty: Beginner, Advanced, Difficult. Puttr Club quests are members only.
  • Ten levels a quest. A level is a fixed list of putts, each from a dealt distance and tee.
  • Three level types: Standard (count the makes), Streak (over at the first miss), Precision (a make only counts inside an entry window that tightens from 90° to 10°).
  • Stars are makes. One, two or three stars at set make counts. One star opens the next level.
  • In Unity today the list and grid were ported as they were, and every level plays on the same 3D ramp under the title "Level N".
Why it does not feel like a game
  • You cannot lose. Outside Streak levels every level runs to the end and hands back zero to three stars. There is nothing to beat.
  • Every level looks the same. Same ramp, same camera, no place, no theme. Level 9 of a Difficult quest looks like level 1 of a Beginner one.
  • A level can only change its putt list. There is no hazard, rule or setting that makes one level play unlike another.
  • Stars buy nothing. Two achievements count finished quests. No reward, no secret, no reason to replay a cleared level.
  • Pace is never asked for. Rollout is the app's pace measure and no quest level uses it.
Rules all three concepts share

Every level is beaten with a real putt

Every putt carries five things a level can use. The camera sees the last 2 ft of the roll, so it reads the make, the entry and the pace. Distance and tee are dealt to the Player and trusted, not checked. No level asks for something the green cannot judge.

What a putt carriesUsed todayWhat it can become in a level
Make or missStandard and Streak levelsClear the obstacle, hit the boss, open the door
Tee (left, centre, right)Dealt, not checkedWhich side you attack from. Never a choice the game has to detect
Distance (3 to 11 ft)Dealt, not checkedHow far the gap, how deep the room
Entry (degrees off centre)Precision levelsGates that narrow, a boss that guards one side, a centre-cut bonus
Pace (rollout past the cup)Not used in questsBridges that need dying pace, speed boosts, ice and sand greens
  • Balls are lives. You carry a bag of balls into a level. A miss costs one. Reach the goal before the bag is empty or the level beats you.
  • Stars stay. Still zero to three a level, so today's saved stars and the two quest achievements carry over.
  • Markers are the coin. A centre-cut make drops a ball marker. Markers buy looks only, the same rule as XP unlocks.
  • Bosses are putting's own villains: the Lip, Three-Putt, the Yips. No borrowed characters.

Screens are drawn at 16:9, the shape of a TV or a tablet propped by the green. Phone layouts follow once a direction is picked.

A
Concept A · world map, stages, bosses

Greenlands

Eight worlds on one map. You walk a path from stage to stage, each stage is a themed green with its own hazard, and a boss guards the way to the next world.

Pick a stage on the map. The path shows what is cleared, what is next and what is still hidden.
Read the rule. One line: make 4 through the blades, 7 balls in the bag.
Putt until you win or run out. A miss costs a ball. Fewer misses, more stars.
Open the path. Stars open secret routes. Six stages open the boss. The boss opens the next world.

Why you come back
  • Secret routes. Three stars on a marked stage opens a hidden bonus green or a shortcut.
  • Boss rematches. A beaten boss can be replayed with a smaller bag for a gold crest.
  • The map fills in. Forty-eight stages and eight bosses, visible from day one.
Build notes
  • Data fits as it is. A quest becomes a world, a quest level becomes a stage, saved results stay stars.
  • Hazards exist already. Windmill and Dead Weight are arcade modes in the Unity app and can dress a stage.
  • New work: the map screen, bag and fail state, boss rules, one scene per world.
B
Concept B · dungeons, keys, items

Under Par

Six dungeons lie under the Old Course, one for each attribute on the Player card: Line, Pace, Short, Mid, Long, Clutch. Each is a maze of rooms. Every room is a putting puzzle, and each dungeon hides an item that changes what you can do next.

Pick a dungeon on the island. Some are open, some need an item you have not found yet.
Work through the rooms. Clear a room to open its doors. Find the key, find the chest.
Use the item. The Metronome shows pace, the Alignment stick shows your start line. Old locked rooms open up.
Beat the boss, take the relic. Six relics complete the Player card crest.

Why you come back
  • Items open old rooms. The Lantern from the Far Gallery lights a dark room in the Still Well. Behind it is a bag piece.
  • Four bag pieces add a ball to your bag for good.
  • Relics show on the Player card, one beside each attribute.
Build notes
  • Six dungeons of about twelve rooms is roughly 70 rooms to design, each by hand.
  • New systems: rooms and doors, keys, items, a saved inventory. None exist in the app or the API.
  • Today's quest tables do not fit. Rooms are a graph, not a list of ten, so this needs its own tables.
  • Six attributes are still an open question in the sports UI doc. This concept leans on them.
C
Concept C · side-scrolling run

Rollout

A side-scroller where every putt is a move. Your ball runs a course of gaps, walls and bonus cups toward a flagstick while a storm closes in from behind. Eight worlds, four courses each, numbered 1-1 to 8-4.

The ball stops at the next obstacle. It shows the putt it wants: 8 ft, right tee.
Make it to get past. Centre cut grabs the marker. Good pace rolls you through the next one too.
Miss and the storm gains. You lose a ball and the storm moves up a step. If it reaches you the course is lost.
Finish on the flagstick. The last putt decides how high you land and what bonus you take.
Obstacles
ObstacleWhat it asks
GapA make at the distance shown. Wider gap, longer putt
WallA make. A fireball breaks it without one
Narrow pipeA make inside an entry window (today's Precision)
Ice bridgeA make at dying pace or it cracks
Bonus cupOptional. Drop in for five putts, ten markers a make
Clubhouse chaseThe fourth course of each world. The storm starts closer and no make can be skipped
Why you come back
  • Marker totals. Every course has a best to beat, so a cleared course still has a score.
  • The fireball run. Clearing a course without losing the streak is its own badge.
  • 100 markers is a ball, so a strong run now pays for a hard course later.
Build notes
  • Data nearly fits. A course is a list of putts, as a quest level is today. Each putt needs an obstacle type added.
  • New work: the 2D course, the runner and its moves, the storm, the flag finish, art for eight worlds.
  • The putt and the picture are apart. You watch your real ball on the real green, then look up to see what it did. A and B draw the green itself, so the screen shows the putt you just hit.

Decision

Build Greenlands, borrow from the other two

Greenlands is the one to build first. It gives the feeling you asked for, a map of levels you have to beat, and it is the only one that sits on what already exists: quests become worlds, levels become stages, saved stars stay, and hazards such as Windmill and Dead Weight are already playable in the Unity app.

  • Take the items from Under Par. Beating a world's boss hands you one item (the Metronome for pace, an entry gauge for entry) that adds a read to the screen from then on. Items only show what the camera sees in the last 2 ft, so there is no start-line item. That gives each world a reward worth more than stars.
  • Take the flag finish from Rollout. Use it as the bonus green behind each secret route: one putt, land as high as you can.
  • Keep Under Par as the sequel. It is the deepest of the three and the most work. If Greenlands lands, the dungeons are a strong second season, and the room rules can be tried first as Greenlands stages.
Decided · 22 November
  1. Direction: Greenlands, with the items from Under Par and the flag finish from Rollout.
  2. Losing: an empty bag ends the level. Retry is free and the bag refills.
  3. Old stars: Greenlands starts clean. Stars from today's quests do not carry in.
  4. Puttr Club: world 1 is open to everyone. Worlds 2 to 8 are Puttr Club only.
  5. The tee: the camera sees only the last 2 ft of a putt, so the game never knows which tee a ball left from. Tees are dealt and trusted. No rule depends on detecting one.
  6. Distances: 3 to 11 ft, all three tees.
